#### Checkpoint H — Commands

### - [x] M43 — Game modes and everyday commands

#### Checkpoint I — v1.3 playtest fixes

Found in Kenzo's v1.3 playtest. Same rules as §7.3 and Checkpoint H. ### - [x] M46 — Spectator, flying and boats
- **Step 0:** list every place that changes a spectator's velocity or position other than input (water drag, current, swimming, block push-out, entity pushing, pistons, collision, gravity, hunger, server corrections) and whether it applies to spectators now. Find why sprinting in water works in spectator (probably the swim state turning on) and what causes the random speed drops: log the spectator's speed every tick for 30 s of flying through water, terrain and mobs on both client and server, and look for where they disagree (a server that simulates spectators with collision or water would rubber-band them). Say what the sprint key does while flying now, and where a boat's bottom sits relative to the water surface at rest.
- **Flying ignores water** (vanilla `isAffectedByFluids()`: a flying player isn't affected by fluids). Spectators, and creative players while flying, get no water drag, no current push, no swimming pose or swim-sprint, no buoyancy, no splash or swim sounds and no air bubbles. Creative players walking in water are still slowed. Underwater fog and tint still render when the eyes are in water.
- **Spectators aren't pushed by anything:** no push-out-of-blocks, no pushing by mobs, players, boats or minecarts, no pistons, slime, honey, soul sand, bubble columns, cobwebs, powder snow or berry bushes, no knockback or explosion velocity. One "no physics" check, not a check in each system.
- **Steady spectator speed:** with input held, speed stays constant at the chosen speed; with none it decays like vanilla flight (×0.91 per tick horizontally, ×0.6 vertically). The server simulates spectators with the same no-physics rules, so it never corrects them. Fix any other cause Step 0 finds.
- **Sprinting while flying, in creative and spectator:** the sprint key (M35 binding, Ctrl by default) and double-tap W work in the air. Flying sprint doubles horizontal flying speed (vanilla flyingSpeed × 2: 0.05 → 0.1 per tick, about 21.6 vs 10.9 blocks/s) and widens the FOV like vanilla. Creative and spectator players can sprint at any hunger and it never drains hunger. In spectator, sprint doubles the mouse-wheel speed (M43). Sprint stops when you stop moving forward; in creative it also stops when you fly into a wall; spectators never collide, so it doesn't stop for them. Ground sprinting in creative is the same as survival.
- **Boats float at vanilla height:** each tick in water, vy = (vy − 0.04 + d × 0.0615) × 0.75 with d = (water surface Y − boat Y) / 0.5625. At rest the bottom settles about 0.37 below the surface and the rim about 0.2 above it (a source block's surface is 8/9 of a block up). Riders sit on the seat, above the water. Render vanilla's water mask: an invisible quad over the inside of the hull, drawn to the depth buffer before translucent water, so no water shows inside a floating boat. Every wood type, the bamboo raft, chest boats, two riders.
- **Tests:** a spectator crossing 10 blocks of water covers the same distance as through air and never enters the swimming state; a spectator inside stone stays put for 100 ticks; a mob, a piston and an explosion don't move a spectator; a server and two clients agree on a spectator's path through water, terrain and mobs with zero corrections; flying sprint covers twice the distance in creative and spectator at hunger 0; a creative player flying through water keeps full speed, walking is slowed; a boat dropped on still water settles within 40 ticks with its bottom 0.33–0.41 below the surface.
- **Done when:** the tests pass and the logged spectator speed over 30 s of flying is steady (min, max and average in the PR). No screenshot; Kenzo checks the feel by hand.

### - [x] M48 — Caves rebuilt to vanilla 1.18+
- **Kenzo's report:** caves aren't like modern Minecraft, a survival player can't get materials, deepslate caves look nothing like vanilla, and there are random boxes of water. M34 was meant to build vanilla's caves; this milestone rebuilds the underground with vanilla's own density formulas and noise settings.
- **Step 0 tools (build these first; they also give the PR's "before" column):**
  - `--cave-test --seed 12345 --chunks 400`: samples 400 chunks over a 4096×4096 area, no rendering, and prints: (a) cave air (air below the surface heightmap) share per 16-block Y band from −64 to 128; (b) cave water and lava per band, and the number of "box" water bodies (connected cave water whose side walls are flat planes on chunk, section or aquifer-cell borders over more than half the perimeter); (c) per column, the depth from the surface to the first cave air, the share of chunks with a cave opening to the sky, and the share of 256×256 areas with at least one; (d) the share of cave air in the largest connected system per 4×4-chunk window; (e) blocks of each ore per chunk (stone and deepslate variants) and exposed ores (a face touching cave air); (f) stone, deepslate and tuff share per band from −64 to 16.
  - `--cave-slice --seed 12345 --x <x> --z <z> --size 256 --out docs/screenshots/cave-slice.png`: a vertical cross-section 256 blocks wide from Y −64 to 128, one pixel per block (stone gray, deepslate dark gray, tuff green-gray, air white, water blue, lava orange, each ore a bright color, the surface as a red line).
  - One line each: how M34 builds caves now, how the water boxes are made, why deepslate caves look wrong, and why ores are hard to reach (wrong Y values, ores that only replace stone so deepslate gets none, ores placed before carving, or too few caves and openings).
- **Density (vanilla NoiseRouterData.overworld final_density):** keep the existing climate splines and the sloped_cheese terrain density. Interpolate the final density over vanilla's 4×8×4-block cells (trilinear). Final density before the slides: if sloped_cheese < 1.5625 → min(sloped_cheese, 5 × entrances) (near the surface only entrances cut in); else → underground(sloped_cheese); then min(that, noodle). underground(s) = max( min( min(cave_layer + cheese, entrances), spaghetti_2d + spaghetti_roughness ), pillars if pillars ≥ 0.03 else −1000000 ), with cave_layer = 4 × noise(cave_layer, xz 1, y 8)² and cheese = clamp(0.27 + noise(cave_cheese, xz 1, y 0.6667), −1, 1) + clamp(1.5 − 0.64 × s, 0, 0.5). Build entrances (spaghetti_3d with rarity and thickness plus cave_entrance with its Y gradient), spaghetti_2d (elevation, thickness modulator, Y gradient), spaghetti_roughness, noodle (Y −60 to 320 only, on where noodle ≥ 0, thickness −0.05 to −0.1, ridge = 1.5 × max(|ridge_a|, |ridge_b|) at 2.6667× frequency) and pillars (25× xz, 0.3× y frequency, rareness and thickness modulated) as vanilla does. Positive is solid. Keep the top slide and the bottom slide (Y −64 to −40 blends to solid).
- **Noise settings** (firstOctave, amplitudes; vanilla "normal noise"): cave_layer −8 [1]; cave_cheese −8 [0.5, 1, 2, 1, 2, 1, 0, 2, 0]; cave_entrance −7 [0.4, 0.5, 1]; spaghetti_3d_1/_2 −7 [1]; spaghetti_3d_rarity −11 [1]; spaghetti_3d_thickness −8 [1]; spaghetti_2d −7 [1]; spaghetti_2d_elevation −8 [1]; spaghetti_2d_modulator −11 [1]; spaghetti_2d_thickness −11 [1]; spaghetti_roughness −5 [1]; spaghetti_roughness_modulator −8 [1]; noodle −8 [1]; noodle_thickness −8 [1]; noodle_ridge_a/_b −7 [1]; pillar −7 [1, 1]; pillar_rareness −8 [1]; pillar_thickness −8 [1]; aquifer_barrier −3 [1]; aquifer_fluid_level_floodedness −7 [1]; aquifer_fluid_level_spread −5 [1]; aquifer_lava −1 [1].
- **Aquifers (vanilla Aquifer.NoiseBasedAquifer), replacing any flat or per-cell water fill:** centers on a grid every 16 blocks in x and z and 12 in y, each jittered (0–9 in x and z, 0–8 in y) by a seeded per-cell random; each block takes the 3 nearest of the surrounding 2×3×2 centers. A center near or above the preliminary surface gets sea level water (Y 63), so oceans, rivers and lakes connect to caves. Otherwise floodedness noise (y × 0.67) decides: above a depth-dependent threshold → flooded to sea level; a middle range → a local level from the spread noise in 3-block steps; below → dry. A local level at or below Y −10 is lava where |aquifer_lava| > 0.3. Below Y −54 the fallback fluid is lava, so open caves from Y −55 down hold lava. Only open blocks below their aquifer's level get fluid. **Barriers:** where the two nearest centers differ in level or fluid, vanilla's pressure (from their distance similarity) + the barrier noise > 0 keeps the block solid, making irregular rock walls between pockets; never a flat wall on a cell, section or chunk border. Fluid at an aquifer's edge next to air gets a fluid update when the chunk loads, so cave waterfalls and lavafalls flow. No cave water above Y 30 unless it connects to the sea.
- **Carvers:** cave carver in 15% of chunks, Y −56 to 180 (y scale 0.1–0.9, floor level −0.7); extra underground cave carver in 7% of chunks, Y −56 to 47; canyon (ravine) carver in 1% of chunks, Y 10 to 67. Carvers ask the aquifer what to place, never cut into the ocean floor under water, and carve lava below Y −55. Drop the old "no carving within 4 blocks below water" rule if aquifers now handle it.
- **Deepslate and underground blocks:** deepslate below Y 0; between Y 0 and 8 each block is deepslate with a chance falling linearly from 100% to 0% (vanilla's vertical_gradient), a noisy blend, not a flat line. Bedrock at Y −64 plus a random gradient up to −60. Deepslate caves are the same noise caves (cheese caverns are most common from about Y −60 to 0), so they're big and open with lava at the bottom. Blobs per chunk: tuff size 64 ×2 from the bottom to Y 0; granite, diorite and andesite size 64 ×2 from Y 0 to 60 plus 1-in-6 chunks from Y 64 to 128; dirt size 33 ×7 from Y 0 to 160; gravel size 33 ×14 from Y −64 to 320; clay in lush caves. Glow lichen, lava lakes, dripstone caves and lush caves (M34) line the new caves.
- **Ores (vanilla 1.20+ OrePlacements: count × vein size, height, discard chance when touching air):** every ore places its deepslate version where it replaces deepslate or tuff and its stone version where it replaces stone, granite, diorite or andesite, and ores are placed AFTER density, aquifers and carvers so caves expose them. Trapezoid = triangle peaking mid-range.
  - Coal: 30 × 17, uniform Y 136..320; 20 × 17, trapezoid Y 0..192, discard 0.5.
  - Iron: 90 × 9, trapezoid Y 80..384; 10 × 9, trapezoid Y −24..56; 10 × 4, uniform Y −64..72.
  - Copper: 16 × 10, trapezoid Y −16..112 (dripstone caves 16 × 20).
  - Gold: 4 × 9, trapezoid Y −64..32, discard 0.5; 0–1 × 9, uniform Y −64..−48, discard 0.5; badlands extra 50 × 9, uniform Y 32..256.
  - Redstone: 4 × 8, uniform Y −64..15; 8 × 8, trapezoid Y −96..−32.
  - Diamond: 7 × 4, trapezoid Y −144..16, discard 0.5; 1-in-9 chunks × 12, same range, discard 0.7; 4 × 8, same range, discard 1.0; 2 × 8, uniform Y −64..−4, discard 0.5.
  - Lapis: 2 × 7, trapezoid Y −32..32; 4 × 7, uniform Y −64..64, discard 1.0.
  - Emerald (mountains only, stone only): 100 × 3, trapezoid Y −16..480.
  - Vein shape is vanilla's OreFeature (an ellipsoid stretched between two points). Remove any leftover M21 squashing; update the ore table in DECISIONS.md and the per-height ore test.
- **Cave entrances follow vanilla,** with no extra suppression (the M34 "keep surface caves rare" rule is dropped).
- **Performance:** chunk generation at most 25% slower than before (cargo bench), and 60+ FPS at render distance 9 while flying over new terrain. Cache per-column values, sample noise only at cell corners, and skip noodle and spaghetti work outside their Y range or toggle before moving on.

### - [x] M49 — Nether basalt and /locate everywhere
- **Step 0:** run --nether-map stats for seed 12345 and print, per biome, basalt pillars and column clusters per chunk, their median and maximum heights, and any outside their biome. - **Basalt pillars (vanilla basalt_pillar):** ONLY in soul_sand_valley, 10 attempts per chunk at random x, z and a uniform Y from 10 to 117, checking the biome at each attempt's position. An attempt places only where its block is air and the block above isn't (it hangs from a ceiling), then places basalt straight down until the first non-air block (lava, fire and plants count, so it never enters the lava sea). Going down, each of the 4 sides gets a basalt block with a 1-in-10 chance per step, stopping on a side once it misses. - **Basalt columns:** small_basalt_columns (4 per floor layer, height 1–4, reach 1) and large_basalt_columns (2 per floor layer, height 5–10, reach 2–3) ONLY in basalt_deltas, per docs/nether-gen.md. ### - [x] M51 — Seeing underwater like modern Minecraft
- **Kenzo's report:** when swimming in an ocean you can barely see 2 blocks ahead, even with Fullbright on. The water's color is fine (keep M47's vanilla colors); the problem is visibility. M47 fixed the color instead, so this milestone fixes what you see with your eyes underwater.
- **Step 0 (measure before changing anything):** with --shot, seed 12345, noon, camera 5 blocks under the surface in a deep ocean looking horizontally along the floor, print: the fog start and end distances actually passed to the shader and their units, the fog formula (linear or exponential, spherical or cylindrical), the water vision value, the opacity of any full-screen underwater overlay, and how many water faces are drawn along the view ray in the 10 blocks in front of the camera. Then the same with Fullbright on. Name the cause in one line. Likely suspects: faces between neighboring water blocks aren't culled (so 10 blocks of water stacks 10+ translucent layers and turns opaque after 2 blocks); the fog end is a tiny number (wrong units or a density used as a distance); the underwater overlay texture (misc/underwater.png) is drawn too opaque; or water is lit as if 15 blocks deep. Fullbright not helping points at fog, layering or the overlay rather than light.
- **Water faces like vanilla:** a water face is drawn only where the neighbor isn't water (and isn't a full opaque block); faces between two water blocks are never drawn. The surface is drawn from below when the camera is underwater. Seen from inside, the water you're in is not a translucent layer at all; only the fog colors it.
- **Underwater fog (vanilla FogRenderer, eyes in water):** linear fog from −8 to fogEnd, where fogEnd = 96 × max(0.25, waterVision), × 0.85 in swamps and mangrove swamps. waterVision: 0 when the eyes aren't in water; under water, with t = ticks since the eyes went under (capped at 600): if t ≥ 600 it's 1, otherwise min(t / 100, 1) × 0.6 + (t < 100 ? 0 : clamp((t − 100) / 500, 0, 1)) × 0.4. So about 24 blocks right away, about 58 after 5 s, 96 after 30 s. Darkness, depth and time of day change only the fog COLOR (the biome water fog color scaled by the light at the eyes), never its distance. Night Vision and Conduit Power brighten the fog color as in vanilla. The same rule applies to the sky, so far geometry fades into the fog color.
- **The underwater overlay:** misc/underwater.png drawn over the screen only while the eyes are in water, at vanilla's low opacity (alpha 0.1, scaled by the light at the eyes), scrolling slightly with camera rotation. If it doesn't exist in any asset layer, draw nothing.
- **Light underwater:** water lowers light by 1 per block (M32), so deep floors are dim but visible at noon, and Fullbright shows everything fully lit. Respiration and Water Breathing change nothing about vision (vanilla).
- **Lava and powder snow** keep their own vanilla fog (lava 0.25 to 1 block, 0 to 3 with Fire Resistance; powder snow 0 to 2), so check the fog change didn't touch them.
- **Tests:** a meshing test where a 10×10×10 water cube produces faces only on its outside; fogEnd equals 24 at t = 0, about 57.6 at t = 100 and 96 at t ≥ 600, and 81.6 at t ≥ 600 in a swamp; fogEnd at midnight equals fogEnd at noon; the underwater overlay's alpha is at most 0.1.
- **Done when:** the tests pass, the Step 0 numbers before and after are in the PR, and the perf test underwater still shows 60+ FPS. One screenshot: underwater in a deep ocean at noon, 5 blocks down, looking along the floor, with the floor visible for dozens of blocks after 30 s under water.
